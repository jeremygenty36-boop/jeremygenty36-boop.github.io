One map among several possible. In my internship I computed these indices with 5 climate datasets. They agree well on the intensity of heat and cold (TXx, TNn: R² of 0.85 to 0.87 between datasets), much less on waves, dry days and SPEI (R² of 0.14 to 0.6). These E-OBS maps are therefore one version of the extremes, not the only one.
Why datasets do not give the same extremes. They are not built the same way. SAFRAN (Météo-France) divides France into 615 supposedly homogeneous zones where only elevation changes the climate. E-OBS interpolates European station data without modelling terrain: its accuracy depends on station density, which is lower in mountains. CHELSA downscales a global reanalysis (ERA5) using terrain, without station observations.

Method

  • Data: E-OBS v31.0e, ensemble mean, daily temperature and precipitation on a 0.1° grid.
  • TXx: maximum of the daily maximum temperature, March to November.
  • TNn: minimum of the daily minimum temperature, September of the previous year to May.
  • HWN: number of heatwaves from May to September, a wave being at least 3 consecutive days above the 90th percentile of Tmax for the calendar day (±7-day window, 1979-1989 reference).
  • CWN: number of cold spells from September to May, at least 3 consecutive days below the 10th percentile of Tmin for the calendar day (same method).
  • WG10P: share of days from March to August when the daily rainfall-minus-evapotranspiration balance falls below its 10th monthly percentile of 1979-1989 (about 10% over the reference).
  • SPEI6: rainfall minus evapotranspiration (Hargreaves) accumulated over 6 months and standardised on the 1981-2010 normal; lowest value from March to August. Negative = drier than normal.
  • 10-year period: a rule specific to each index, computed cell by cell: maximum for TXx, minimum for TNn and SPEI6, total for HWN and CWN, mean for WG10P. An extreme is never averaged.
  • Trend: slope of a 1979-2024 linear regression, per decade. Cells where the trend is not significant (p ≥ 0.05) are faded.
  • Missing values: a season with more than 10% missing days is left empty. No value is clipped.

Method taken from my Master's internship on climate extremes and tree mortality (UMR Silva), where five climate datasets were compared. Code: internship repository.
